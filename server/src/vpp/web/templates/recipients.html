Before you start

Setup takes about 15 minutes. Payments go straight into your own Stripe account, so Stripe needs to know who you are. Here's what to have ready, so nothing surprises you halfway through.

What happens, step by step

  1. Set up Stripe

    Open the link we send you. You create a Stripe login with two-step verification, and Stripe checks your identity and bank details. We never see your ID or bank details.

  2. Add a passkey

    Back on {{ brand() }}, add a passkey: your phone's or laptop's fingerprint or face unlock. That's how you sign in and sign for payments. No password to remember.

  3. Prove your website

    Publish the small file we show you at /.well-known/vpp.json on your site. From then on, the payments you sign show as confirmed by your domain.

Good to know

Why Stripe asks so much

Payments land in your own Stripe account, not ours, so Stripe treats you as the business receiving them. EU anti-money-laundering rules require it to verify you. In exchange, we can never hold, delay or lose your money.

What it costs

Nothing from us: {{ brand() }} takes no cut and has no monthly fee. Stripe charges its normal card fees. Donors can add an optional tip for {{ brand() }} on top of their donation. Stripe's fee is calculated on the whole payment, so a €2 tip adds about 3 cents to your Stripe fee.

If the identity check fails

Usually it's a typo in your name or date of birth. Use the same setup link to correct it. If Stripe then asks for a photo ID and selfie, that's normal and only happens once.

What becomes public

Your profile name, your domain, and the payments you received, with amounts and dates. Payers' names appear only if they choose to share them. Your ID and bank details stay with Stripe.

Do I need crypto?

No. You're paid in euros into your Stripe account, like any card payment. {{ brand() }} timestamps a fingerprint of the ledger in Bitcoin every hour so nobody can rewrite it later, but no bitcoin changes hands and you never touch it. How that works.
